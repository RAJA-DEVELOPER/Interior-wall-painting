The Definitive Guide to Venetian Plaster in the Modern Home

Everything you need to know — from what it is and how it's applied, to costs, maintenance and how to find a specialist you can trust.

What Is Venetian Plaster?

Venetian plaster — also known as stucco veneziano — is a wall finish made from slaked lime putty mixed with marble dust. It originated in Venice during the Renaissance period and has been used by the world's finest craftspeople ever since. When applied by a skilled hand, it produces a surface of extraordinary luminosity, depth and texture that no paint or wallpaper can replicate.

Unlike paint, which sits on the surface, Venetian plaster becomes the wall. It creates a finish that appears to have depth — as though you are looking into rather than at the surface. In direct or indirect light, it has an almost mineral quality.

"Venetian plaster is not a finish — it is a transformation. Applied well, it turns a wall into something that feels like it belongs to the building itself."

— Dmitri Volkov, Evora's Venetian Plaster Specialist

The Application Process: What to Expect

A true Venetian plaster finish typically requires three to four coats, each applied and burnished by hand. The process cannot be rushed — each coat must be fully dry before the next is applied, and the final burnishing determines the level of sheen.

  • Preparation: All surfaces must be perfectly smooth and primed. Any imperfections in the wall will be visible in the final finish, so we always skim-coat walls before beginning.
  • First coat: A base layer is applied and left to dry. This typically takes 4–8 hours depending on temperature and humidity.
  • Second and third coats: Progressively thinner coats are applied at different angles to build depth and variation.
  • Burnishing: The final coat is burnished with a Venetian steel trowel while the plaster is still slightly wet, creating the characteristic polished, marble-like surface.
  • Waxing (optional): A natural beeswax or carnauba wax can be applied to increase the sheen and provide additional protection.

Is Venetian Plaster Right for Your Home?

Venetian plaster is suitable for almost any room, but it works best in spaces where you spend time appreciating the walls — dining rooms, sitting rooms, bedrooms and entrance halls. It is particularly beautiful in rooms with natural light that moves across the surface through the day.

It is not generally recommended for bathrooms or kitchens unless waxed or sealed properly, as moisture can affect the finish over time. For wet areas, a silicone or acrylic-based Venetian plaster is available.

The Cost of Venetian Plaster

Venetian plaster is a premium finish, and pricing reflects the skill and time required. At Evora, our Venetian plaster typically costs between £45–£85 per square metre, depending on the complexity of the finish, the colour and the level of burnishing required.

A typical dining room feature wall of approximately 12–14m² would cost between £540 and £1,190. A full four-wall dining room would range from £1,800 to £3,400. We provide fully itemised, fixed-price quotes after an in-home consultation.

How to Choose a Venetian Plaster Specialist

The quality of Venetian plaster varies enormously depending on the skill of the applicator. A poorly applied Venetian plaster will look flat, patchy and unremarkable. A master-applied finish is breathtaking. When choosing a specialist:

  • Ask to see completed project photography — ideally in multiple lighting conditions
  • Request references from recent clients
  • Ask about their training and how long they have been applying Venetian plaster specifically
  • Request a small sample panel in your chosen colour before committing
